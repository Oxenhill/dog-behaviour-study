# Core Metric 5: Broader Behavioural-Problem Prevalence (beyond bites)
> **NEW Session 8, per explicit user instruction: "think bigger than just bites."** See the SCOPE BROADENING note in _PROJECT_STATE.md. This file holds Core Metric 5 -- owner/clinical-reported prevalence of the FULL range of canine behaviour problems (separation-related behaviour, fear/anxiety, aggression in all its forms not just biting, noise sensitivity, compulsive behaviour, disobedience/excitability) -- as a genuinely distinct metric from Core Metric 2 (bite incident rates specifically). Cross-reference this file from each country's bite/referral sections rather than duplicating content.

---

## THE central methodological finding of this file: prevalence figures are NOT comparable across studies without knowing the measurement threshold

Before any cross-country reading of the numbers below, this must be understood: unlike bites (a discrete, relatively well-defined event -- an animal's teeth broke skin or didn't), "behaviour problem prevalence" is enormously sensitive to HOW the question is asked and what counts as a "problem." The four studies below, ranked by headline prevalence:

| Country | Headline "has a behaviour problem" prevalence | What was actually measured |
|---------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| USA (Dog Aging Project / Beaver 2024-25) | **99.12%** | ANY non-zero score (i.e. score >0 out of a 0-4 severity scale) on ANY of 28 behaviour questions counts as "exhibiting a behaviour response." The study's own author cautions this means "dogs do not have a lot of problems, and the problem behaviours that do exist are relatively minor" -- the headline number is a measurement-sensitivity artifact, not a claim that 99% of US dogs have a serious problem. |
| Finland (Salonen/Sulkama et al. 2020) | **72.5%** | Owner-reported presence of ANY of 7 anxiety-like traits (noise sensitivity, fearfulness, fear of surfaces/heights, inattention/impulsivity, compulsion, separation-related behaviour, aggression) at a clinically-informed threshold, not a bare nonzero response. |
| Denmark (Meyer et al. 2023) | **34%** | Owner's own SUBJECTIVE judgement -- "do you perceive your dog to have one or more problems related to behaviour" -- a stricter, self-selecting threshold than either of the above. |
| Australia (VetCompass, young-dog mortality subset) | **29.7% of DEATHS** (not of the living population) | A completely different denominator -- this is the share of confirmed deaths in dogs =<3yo attributable to undesirable-behaviour-linked causes (euthanasia, dog attack, road-traffic accidents linked to behaviour), not a prevalence-in-the-living-population figure at all. Not directly comparable to the other three rows above. |

**This variance is itself the single most important Phase 5 finding candidate in this file** -- a 3x spread (34% to 99%) across genuinely comparable Western countries almost certainly reflects measurement-threshold differences, not real underlying differences in dog behaviour. Any cross-country claim built on these headline numbers without carrying this caveat forward would be a serious analytical error. Where possible, prefer the SPECIFIC sub-category breakdowns (e.g. "% of dogs with separation-related behaviour") over the headline "any problem" percentage, since narrower categories are somewhat more comparable, though still not on a standardised instrument across all four countries.

| Any anxiety-like trait present | 72.5% | 2020 | -- |
| Noise sensitivity | 32% | 2020 | Most common single trait |
| Fearfulness (general) | 29% | 2020 | -- |
| Fear of surfaces/heights | 23.5% | 2020 | -- |
| Inattention/impulsivity | ~20% | 2020 | -- |
| Compulsive behaviour | 16% | 2020 | -- |
| Hyperactivity/impulsivity | 15% | 2020 | -- |
| Aggression (any form, any target) | 14% | 2020 | Directly comparable in TYPE (not necessarily in method) to Denmark's dog-directed/stranger-directed aggression rows below |
| Separation-related behaviour | 5-6% | 2020 | Notably LOWER than the USA's 85.9% "separation and attachment" figure and Denmark's disobedience-adjacent figures -- another illustration of definitional non-comparability (Finland's is a narrower clinical separation-anxiety-type definition, not a broad "attachment behaviours" category) |

**Comorbidity finding:** dogs with separation-related behaviour were 4.1x more likely to also show hyperactivity/impulsivity and 3.4x more likely to show inattention -- suggests these are not independent problems, a relevant caveat against simply summing category percentages.

## Denmark

**Source: Meyer et al. 2023, Journal of Veterinary Behavior -- [quality: A, peer-reviewed, described as a representative sample of Danish dog owners] -- [Scope: household-only]**

**Sample-size discrepancy flagged, both figures logged per the data-integrity rule:** the University of Copenhagen's own summary (animalethics.ku.dk) states n=502; a secondary summary (companionanimalpsychology.com) states 5,001+ respondents aged 18-89. These may refer to different stages of the same study (e.g. a larger initial recruitment panel vs. a smaller behaviour-specific sub-sample) -- NOT reconciled this session, logged as-is.

| Metric | Figure | Year | Notes |
|--------|--------|------|-------|
| Sample size | 502 (per KU) or 5,001+ (per secondary summary) | 2023 | See discrepancy note above |
| Owners perceiving their dog to have any behaviour problem | 34% (n=172 of 502) | 2023 | Subjective owner-judgement threshold |
| Fear-related issues | 18% | 2023 | -- |
| Disobedience | 11% | 2023 | -- |
| Fear of noises | 10% | 2023 | -- |
| Inter-dog aggression | 8% | 2023 | -- |
| Fear of other dogs | 7% | 2023 | From the secondary summary's breakdown -- may partially overlap with "fear-related issues" above rather than being fully additive |
| Aggression toward unfamiliar people | 3% | 2023 | -- |
| Aggression toward guardian | <1% | 2023 | -- |

**Two findings of direct significance to this study's core management-culture hypothesis:**
1. Higher training frequency correlated with LOWER risk across fear, aggression, and disobedience categories.
2. MORE daily off-leash time was associated with REDUCED aggression and disobedience -- this is a striking, specific, quantitative link between exactly the kind of management-culture variable already built in variables/management-practices-and-culture.md (Denmark's leash/off-leash regime) and a behavioural outcome. This is a genuine candidate finding for Phase 5, not just background -- it should be read alongside Denmark's BSL null result and its hybrid seasonal/zoned leash-exception structure (both already documented in this project).
3. Apartment living was associated with HIGHER fear-problem prevalence than houses/farms -- a housing-density variable not yet tracked elsewhere in this study, flagged as a possible new variable candidate.
4. **Methodological point of wide relevance:** the study's author (Dr. Meyer) noted owner-reported behaviour-problem prevalence was "much higher than what is found in veterinary records" -- direct evidence that CLINICAL/administrative behaviour data (of the kind most of this study's Core Metrics 1-3 rely on) substantially UNDER-counts true prevalence relative to survey data. This should be stated explicitly in the final report as a limitation applying to every clinical/shelter/referral figure in this dataset, not just Denmark's.

| Metric | Figure | Period | Notes |
|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Underlying database size | 933,353 dogs, 157 clinics | 2013-2018 | VetCompass Australia primary-care sentinel network |
| Dogs =<3yo confirmed deceased in sub-study | 4,341 | 2013-2018 | -- |
| Share of those deaths attributed to undesirable behaviour (UB) | 29.7% (1,160 dogs = 26.7% of ALL deaths in the sample) | 2013-2018 | Different denominator to the USA/Finland/Denmark prevalence-in-living-population figures -- see the comparability warning at the top of this file |
| UB deaths from road traffic accidents | 41.4% of UB deaths | 2013-2018 | Behaviour (e.g. escaping, chasing, lack of recall) as an indirect cause of death |
| UB deaths from dog attack (by/on another dog) | 16.8% of UB deaths | 2013-2018 | -- |
| Most commonly reported UB category overall | Aggression | 2013-2018 | Anxiety/nervousness also frequently documented |
| **Referral rate (Core Metric 3 -- directly relevant)** | Only 11.0% of UB-linked cases were referred to a behaviourist/trainer | 2013-2018 | 5.9% received pharmacological therapy; 82.8% had NO documented treatment at all |
| Risk factors | Neutered dogs 2.5x odds vs intact; crossbred 1.43x vs purebred; Australian Cattle Dog (OR 4.77) and American Staffordshire Terrier (OR 4.69) highest-risk breeds | 2013-2018 | -- |

**Significance and correction:** the 11%-referred figure itself was ALREADY logged in an earlier session (as PMC7918417 in data/by-country/Australia.md) -- it is the same VetCompass Australia study, reached this session via its MDPI mirror. What this session adds is the surrounding context that was NOT previously extracted: the 82.8%-no-documented-treatment figure, the 29.7%-of-deaths headline, and the road-traffic-accident/dog-attack cause breakdown. Australia.md has been corrected to reflect this rather than presenting the referral rate itself as a new find. It remains genuinely one of the thinnest, most valuable data points in the whole study (referral rate is "Gap" or near-Gap for almost every other country).

## Data Gaps
- No standardised single-instrument (e.g. pure C-BARQ) comparison exists yet across more than one or two of this study's countries -- the four entries above come from four different survey instruments/thresholds, which is precisely the comparability problem flagged at the top of this file.
- USA's primary peer-reviewed papers not yet directly extracted (robots-blocked / rate-limited twice this session).
- Denmark's sample-size discrepancy (502 vs 5,001+) not resolved.
- No time-series/trend data yet for any of the four countries above (all are single-point studies) -- unlike bite/BSL data, this metric currently has no before/after or year-over-year view anywhere in the dataset.
