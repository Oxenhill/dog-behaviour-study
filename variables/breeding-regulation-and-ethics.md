## United Kingdom
| Date | Change | Detail | Source |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| 1 Oct 2018 | Animal Welfare (Licensing of Activities Involving Animals) (England) Regulations 2018 in force | Introduced a graded (1-5 star) licensing system for commercial dog breeders in England, replacing the 1973 Breeding of Dogs Act framework | legislation.gov.uk (primary legislative text) -- [quality: A] |
| 6 Apr 2020 | Lucy's Law in force (England) | Bans commercial third-party sales of puppies/kittens -- buyers must go direct to a breeder or a rescue; unlicensed sellers face unlimited fine or up to 6 months imprisonment | UK Government (DEFRA media blog, 6 Apr 2020 post) -- [quality: A, primary government source] |

**Significance:** England moved from a low-enforcement 1973 framework to a graded licensing + point-of-sale ban within an 18-month window (Oct 2018-Apr 2020), immediately before the pandemic-era ownership surge (2020-2022) documented in variables/population-density-and-ownership.md and UK.md. This timing is analytically important: the tightened breeder framework and the acquisition surge happened almost simultaneously, so any link between "better regulation" and outcomes in this specific window is confounded by the concurrent demand shock -- flagged for the Phase 5 analysis, not resolved here.

## United States
| Date | Change | Detail | Source |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Ongoing (no landmark date located) | USDA federal licensing threshold | Federal license required ONLY if a breeder has more than 4 breeding females AND sells wholesale/sight-unseen/through brokers or pet stores. Breeders with <=4 females, or who sell retail direct-to-consumer (including via the breeder's own website, a major post-2010 sales channel), fall outside federal oversight entirely | ASPCA, "Federal Licensing & USDA Standards" -- [quality: B, advocacy org citing USDA rules] |
| 2010 | Federal watchdog report | Documented USDA enforcement failures in the existing licensing regime | ASPCA (citing federal audit) -- [quality: B] |
| 2024 | Updated federal audit | Again documented enforcement/oversight problems at USDA | ASPCA (citing federal audit) -- [quality: B] |

**Significance:** the USA has the least comprehensive breeder regulation of any country in this study -- no national registration mandate for the large "retail direct" and small-scale segments of the industry, which is structurally distinct from the UK/Germany/Austria/Netherlands model of universal registration + licensing. This is a genuine structural difference worth carrying into Phase 5, not just a data gap.

## Scandinavia (Sweden / Norway / Finland / Denmark)
**Structural note (not a single dated timeline -- the regulatory model itself differs from continental Europe):** breeding standards in the Nordic countries are largely governed through national **kennel club** frameworks (Svenska Kennelklubben in Sweden, Suomen Kennelliitto/Finnish Kennel Club, etc.) rather than direct government statute. This is a genuinely different regulatory architecture from Germany/Austria/Netherlands (government-mandated registration and welfare law) or the UK (government licensing since 2018) -- worth carrying into Phase 5 as a structural variable in its own right, not merely a data gap. **No specific effective-date timeline located this session for government-level Nordic breeding law -- flagged as a gap for a future pass if government (as opposed to kennel-club) regulation is later found.**
