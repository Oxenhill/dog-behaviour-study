# United States — Data File

## Dog Bite Incident Data

**Source quality: A (CDC/AHRQ/government primary data)**

| Metric | Figure | Year | Source |
|--------|--------|------|--------|
| Total annual bites estimated | 4.5 million | 2001–2003 (most recent CDC survey) | CDC / Sacks et al., Injury Prevention 2008 |
| Seeking medical care | ~885,000/yr | ~2022 | AVMA/CDC |
| Emergency department visits | 395,000 | 2022 (highest on record) | HCUP NEDS / WISQARS |
| Hospitalisations | ~12,000–14,000/yr | 2018–2023 | HCUP; ~4 per 100,000 population |
| ER visit rate increase | +15% | 2018–2023 | DogsBite.org from WISQARS; COVID-year drop then recovery |
| Fatalities | 127 | 2024 | CDC WONDER (provisional) |
| Fatalities | 98 | 2022 | CDC WONDER |
| Fatalities avg 2018–2023 | ~70/yr | — | CDC WONDER |
| Fatalities avg 2005–2018 | ~33.6/yr | — | CDC WONDER |
| Fatality rate | ~0.03–0.04 per 100,000 | 2022–2024 | Calculated from CDC data |

**Key note:** The 4.5 million total bite figure is from 2001–2003 data and has not been updated by a national survey — it is widely cited but the methodology has not been repeated. ER visit data from WISQARS is the most current repeatable metric. Rural areas have 4× higher bite injury rates than urban areas (AHRQ 2010).

**Sudden shift flagged:** Fatal dog attacks nearly doubled post-COVID: avg 33.6/yr (2005–2018) → avg 70/yr (2018–2023) → 127 in 2024. Potential causation: pandemic pet acquisition surge (2020–2021), acquisition of dogs without appropriate socialisation/training, post-pandemic lifestyle changes (reduced supervision, more off-property time). This requires further investigation.

**Insurance data (supplementary):**
- 2024: $1.57 billion paid, 22,658 claims, average $69,272 per claim
- 2022: $1.136 billion
- Cost per claim increased 209.2% from 2016–2025 (Insurance Information Institute)
- Source: Insurance Information Institute, annual Spotlight on Dog Bite Liability

**Population scope note (bite data):** CDC WISQARS/MMWR fatality data and HCUP ER/hospitalisation data are NOT restricted by ownership status — they record all reported dog bites/attacks regardless of whether the dog was owned, stray, feral, or a working/security dog. The US has a modest but real free-roaming and community-dog population (parts of the rural South, some tribal lands), so this data is tagged **[Scope: mixed/unclear]**, not assumed household-only. By contrast, USPS carrier-bite data and Insurance Information Institute liability claims ARE **[Scope: household-only]** by construction — a liability claim requires an identified policyholder's dog, and carrier bites are logged at private residences.

## Shelter Surrender / Relinquishment Data

**Source: Shelter Animals Count (SAC) national database — A grade**

| Metric | Figure | Year | Source |
|--------|--------|------|--------|
| Total dog/cat shelter intakes | ~5.8 million | 2024 | SAC 2025 Annual Report |
| Owner surrenders (% of all intakes) | 29–30% | 2024–2025 | SAC |
| Stray intakes (% of all intakes) | 59% | 2025 | SAC |
| Dog adoptions | ~2 million | 2025 | SAC/ASPCA |
| Dogs euthanised | ~320,000 | 2025 | SAC/ASPCA |
| Euthanasia rate | ~8% of all intakes | 2025 (down from 10% in 2019) | SAC |
| Dog intakes decline since 2019 | -11% (735,000 fewer) | 2019–2024 | SAC |

**Behaviour as surrender reason:**
- 28% of owner surrenders cited behaviour issues as primary reason (Kisley et al. 2024, 2018–2023, US open-admission shelter, n=2,836 records) — *Source: Animals, MDPI doi:10.3390/ani14172606*
- 40% of relinquished dogs had at least one behavioural reason (NCPPSP Regional Shelter Study, 12 shelters, n=1,984) — *Source: Salman et al. 2000, J Applied Animal Welfare Science 3(2)*
- 7.8% specifically aggressive behaviour as cited reason — *Source: ASPCA data cited in The Zebra*
- 14.1% housing issues (top single reason for dogs) — *Source: ASPCA*
- 65% of owners in one California study cited behaviour playing "at least some part" — *Source: Kwan & Bain*
- In a US/Canada sample: Behavioural Incompatibility class = 43/452 (9.5%) of surrendering owners — *Source: ResearchGate, risk factors study*
- Relinquishment rate (% of dog population entering shelters annually): estimated ~6.1–7.1% — *Source: Woodruff & Smith 2019, modelling study*

**Definition note:** "Behaviour" as a surrender reason is inconsistently defined across studies. Some studies lump all behaviour categories; others list aggression, fearfulness, house soiling separately. Direct comparison with other countries is not possible without aligned definitions.

**Confounders:**
- Socioeconomic status strongly associated with relinquishment risk
- Lower-income owners, renters, those without obedience class participation at higher risk
- 10% of owner surrenders linked to financial reasons (HASS 2025 Human Commonalities Project)
- Housing restrictions (no-pet clauses) are the #1 stated single reason for dog surrender

**Population scope note (shelter data):** Shelter Animals Count distinguishes intake type. The 'owner surrender' category is **[Scope: household-only]** by definition. The 59% 'stray' intake category is NOT necessarily non-household — a substantial but unquantified share of 'stray' intakes nationally are lost/escaped owned pets rather than truly ownerless dogs, and SAC's national aggregate does not separate the two, so it is tagged **[Scope: mixed/unclear]**. The behaviour-as-surrender-reason statistics above (Kisley 2024, Salman 2000/NCPPSP, ASPCA) all specifically study owner-initiated surrenders and are **[Scope: household-only]**.

## Behaviour Referral / Professional Data

**Source availability: LOW for national rates**
- Case distribution at one university veterinary behaviour referral service: 1,923 cases over 20 years (1997–2017), 72.2% aggression, 20.1% anxiety/fears — *Source: PMC8909650*
- No published national rate of referral to behaviour professionals per dog population
- **Gap flagged:** No US national statistic on behaviour case rate as % of dog-owning households

**Population scope note (referral data):** **[Scope: household-only]** — behaviour referral, by definition, applies to client-owned dogs presented to a veterinary or behaviour-referral service.

## Data Gaps
- National bite survey not repeated since 2001–2003
- Behaviour referral rate not tracked nationally
- No national shelter reason coding standard (SAC records intake type but not reason detail nationally)
