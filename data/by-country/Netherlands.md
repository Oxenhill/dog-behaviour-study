## Dog Bite Incident Data

**Primary source: Cornelissen & Hopster 2010, Veterinary Journal — B grade (older, internet survey)**
**Fatality data: Sarenbo & Svensson 2021, Forensic Science International — A grade (Eurostat ICD-10)**

| Metric | Figure | Year | Source |
|--------|--------|------|--------|
| Annual bite incidents (all severities) | ~136,000 | ~2008–2010 | Cornelissen & Hopster 2010 |
| Bite rate (all severities) | 8.3 per 1,000 population (= 830 per 100,000) | ~2008–2010 | Cornelissen & Hopster 2010 |
| Treated at GP | ~40,000/yr | ~2008–2010 | Cornelissen & Hopster 2010 |
| Treated at ED/polyclinic | ~11,000/yr | ~2008–2010 | Cornelissen & Hopster 2010 |

| Hospitalisation rate | ~1.7 per 100,000 (population ~17.5M) | ~2008–2010 | Calculated |

**Critical comparison note:**
The Netherlands' 830 per 100,000 figure (all bites) appears high, but the hospitalisation rate (~1.7 per 100,000) is dramatically lower than UK (~22/100,000) or Australia (36/100,000). This reflects:
1. Different measurement (all reported bites vs hospital admissions only)
2. Different healthcare routing (Netherlands uses GP as first point; most bites treated there)
3. The 2010 data is not comparable to 2023/24 UK/Australia data — it predates the pandemic surge

**Data gap flagged:** No equivalent Netherlands study covering 2015–2024. Current hospitalisation data for Netherlands not located. The Cornelissen & Hopster study is the primary published source for Dutch bite incidence. More recent Dutch government health data not found in Phase 1 search.

**Fatality data (Europe-wide, Eurostat ICD-10):**
- Sarenbo & Svensson 2021: Netherlands not listed individually in top fatality countries
- European average: 0.009 per 100,000 (2016)
- Hungary highest in Europe (94 deaths 1995–2016); France 79; UK 56; Poland 49
- Source: *Bitten or struck by dog: A rising number of fatalities in Europe, 1995–2016*, Forensic Science International 2020

**Population scope note (bite data):** Cornelissen & Hopster 2010 is an internet survey and does not itself restrict responses to household-owned dogs (a bite from a neighbour's or stranger's dog would still be reported). However, the Netherlands has an independently verified, government-policy-driven zero (or near-zero) free-roaming dog population, so the underlying dog population being sampled from is functionally household-only. Tagged **[Scope: household-only, verified by national policy context rather than by the source's own methodology]**.

## Shelter / Stray Dog Data

**Source: Multiple — A grade for zero stray dog status (government policy, independently verified)**

| Metric | Detail | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Stray dog population | Zero (effectively) | Multiple verified sources; Dutch government policy |
| Shelter policy | No-kill mandatory | Dutch law |
| Dog population | ~1.7-1.8 million (FEDIAF 2022: 1.83M/18% households; Dibevo/NVG survey: 1.8M in 2021 declining to 1.7M by 2025) | FEDIAF Facts & Figures 2022; Dibevo/NVG survey via GlobalPETS — supersedes prior session's rough ~1M estimate, see variables/population-density-and-ownership.md |
| Dog ownership rate | One of highest in EU | FEDIAF |
| Dog microchipping | Mandatory | Dutch law |
| Municipal registration | Mandatory | Dutch law |

**Why Netherlands cannot be compared directly on shelter surrender rates:**
The Netherlands has eliminated the stray dog problem entirely through a multi-decade policy combining: mandatory microchipping, mandatory registration, municipal animal police, CNVR programme, no-kill shelter policy, and financial incentives (shelter adoption over purchase). This means:
- Surrender data reflects owner-relinquished dogs only, not strays
- No-kill policy means behaviour-related euthanasia does not occur (unlike US/UK/Australia)
- This is a fundamentally different system architecture — behaviour surrender data exists but in a different context

**Behaviour-related surrender rate in NL shelters:**
- A Utrecht University thesis (Hermsen 2012 research programme) investigated behaviour problems in shelter dogs — Dutch shelters DO receive behaviour-problem dogs but breakdown not accessible as published aggregate data
- Hondenbescherming (Dutch animal protection) runs this research — contact institution for current data

**Population scope confirmation (shelter data):** **[Scope: household-only]** — confirmed above; the Netherlands' no-stray policy architecture means shelter/relinquishment data by construction describes owner-relinquished household dogs only.

## Breed Specific Legislation

**Notable:** Netherlands repealed BSL (Pit Bull ban) in **2009**, following evidence that it had not reduced bite incidence.
- The pre-2009 Dutch BSL was one of the most studied repeals in European literature
- Post-repeal, bite statistics did not significantly worsen — cited as evidence against BSL efficacy
- Source: Cornelissen & Hopster 2010 (same study)
