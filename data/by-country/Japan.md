# Japan — Data File

## Dog Bite Incident Data

**Primary source: Ministry of Environment Animal Welfare and Management Office — A grade (government)**

| Metric | Figure | Year | Source |
|--------|--------|------|--------|
| Serious dog bite incidents reported | 4,208 | 2015 | Japan MoE Animal Welfare and Management Office |
| Of which: fatalities (non-family) | 2 | 2015 | Japan MoE |
| Of which: bites involving owners/family | ≤5% of total | 2015 | Japan MoE |
| Calculated rate (serious bites) | ~3.4 per 100,000 | 2015 | Calculated: 4,208 / 125M population |

**Critical definition note:** "Serious dog bite incidents" (重大事故) in Japan refers to incidents reported to local government health authorities — this captures only the most severe/formally reported bites. This is NOT comparable to UK hospital admission data or US medical-care-seeking data. Japanese bite incidence is almost certainly significantly underreported.

**Data gap flagged:** No equivalent of NHS HES or AIHW data for Japan. Hospital admission data for dog bites in Japan is not published in accessible English-language sources.

**Population scope note (bite data):** Japan MoE 'serious incident' reporting does not specify ownership status. Japan has a very small but non-zero free-roaming dog/cat population in some rural areas historically. Tagged **[Scope: mixed/unclear]**.

## Shelter / Euthanasia Data

**Primary source: Japan Ministry of Environment — A grade**

| Metric | Figure | Year | Source |
|--------|--------|------|--------|
| Dogs and cats culled nationally | ~205,000 | 2010 | Japan MoE |
| Shelter system | Hokensho (public health centres) — historically used gas chambers | Pre-2012 | Japan Today / confirmed by multiple sources |
| Trend | Significant decline in culling rates as animal welfare awareness increased | 2010–2024 | MoE trend data; Statista lists "dogs and cats brought to prefectural shelter facilities 2014–2023" (behind paywall) |

**Cultural context (important variable):**
- Japan has very few independent rehoming shelters (unlike UK/US)
- When owners surrender to hokensho, animals historically faced euthanasia
- 2012 legislation strengthened animal welfare; euthanasia in gas chambers being phased out
- Cultural attitudes toward dogs changing significantly over study period: dogs increasingly viewed as family members

**Definition issue:** Japan's system does not distinguish "behaviour surrender" from other reasons in the same way Western countries do. The hokensho system functions differently from RSPCA/Dogs Trust model.

**Academic context:**
- PMC comparative analysis notes Japan included in international dog owner education studies
- Municipally-led dog owner education programmes emphasise "public responsibility" and "community coexistence"
- Source: *A Comparative Analysis of International Dog Owner Education Programmes*, PMC12897032

**Population scope note (shelter data):** **[Scope: mixed/unclear, with a temporal dimension]** — the hokensho (public health centre) system historically combined owner-surrendered dogs and captured stray/found dogs without separating the two nationally. Because stray capture has declined significantly since the 2010s as Japan's free-roaming population fell, the mix is likely shifting toward owner-surrender-dominant in more recent years — but no published breakdown confirms this, so it must be treated as a stated gap, not assumed.
