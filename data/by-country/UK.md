**Primary source: NHS Hospital Episode Statistics (HES) — A grade**
**Academic analysis: Tulloch et al. 2021, Scientific Reports — A grade**

| Metric | Figure | Year | Source |
|--------|--------|------|--------|

| Admission rate | 14.99 per 100,000 | 2018 | Tulloch et al. 2021 |

| A&E attendances (estimated) | ~207,000/yr | ~2023 | Estimated from NHS data |
| Fatalities | 2 | 2019 | ONS |
| Fatalities | 5–6 | 2022 | ONS |
| Fatalities | 15–20 | 2023 | ONS / British Brief |
| NHS direct cost (admissions peak) | £25.1 million | 2017/18 | Tulloch et al. 2021 |
| NHS A&E cost (peak) | £45.7 million | 2017/18 | Tulloch et al. 2021 |

**Sudden shift flagged:** 
- Fatalities: 2 (2019) → 20+ (2023) — 800%+ increase over 4 years
- Admissions: +33% in single year (2022/23 to 2023/24)
- Likely causation: Pandemic puppy acquisition surge (2020–21) with estimated 3.2 million extra dogs acquired; many inexperienced first-time owners; breeds unsuited to household life; XL Bully incidents prominently in 2023 triggering legislation February 2024
- Dog population: 9 million → 13.5 million (2019 to 2022 estimate; PFMA/Dogs Trust)

**Key notes:**
- Rise driven by adults, not children (Tulloch et al. 2021: tripling in adult incidence)
- Deprivation gradient: 3× higher admission rate in most deprived vs least deprived areas (NHS Digital 2014)
- Geographic variation: Merseyside highest (32.2/100,000), Kent/Medway lowest (7.3/100,000) in 2014/15 data

**Definition used:** "Bitten or struck by a dog" — ICD-10 external cause code W54, NHS HES Finished Consultant Episodes (FCEs). This is hospital admission only; does not include GP visits or minor injuries. Comparable to Australian AIHW hospitalization coding.

**Post-pandemic note:** "Post-pandemic return crisis" — shelter intake rose 18% in 2024/25 as pandemic puppies reached adolescence; behavioural issues (separation anxiety, reactivity) cited as top reason — Source: Petz.uk UK Pet Statistics 2026 citing PDSA/RSPCA data

**Confounder note:** Dogs Trust specifically attributes rising complex cases partly to cost-of-living crisis and post-pandemic ownership patterns. The 6.07% figure for "unwanted behaviours" from Dogs Trust 2024 and the 34.2% from Diesel et al. 2008 appear discrepant — this reflects different methodology (Dogs Trust: reason given at point of contact; Diesel: academic coding of behaviour contribution to decision). Both figures are valid but measure different things.

**PDSA PAW Report data (supplementary):**
- 2024: 15% of dog owners used at least one aversive training method; 6% used two — PDSA PAW Report 2024
- Correlation reported between aversive training and behaviour concerns — PDSA PAW Report 2024
- UK dog population: 10.6 million (2024) — PDSA/YouGov nationally representative survey

## Behaviour Referral / Professional Data

**Source availability: MODERATE — survey data, no national case rate**
- >50% of companion animal practices in Ireland have referred cases to a behaviour specialist — Duggan et al. 2018, Irish Vet Journal
- Referral pathways in UK: ABTC (Animal Behaviour and Training Council) register, APBC (Association of Pet Behaviour Counsellors), CCAB (Certified Clinical Animal Behaviourist)
- No regulation of dog trainers in UK (voluntary registration only)
- **Gap flagged:** No published national rate of behaviour case referrals per dog population in UK
